# AI literacy at work

Needs the MEXTY MCP. For schools, use `ai-literacy-curriculum`.

## Start from what people do

AI literacy is not one course for everyone. Ask:

- **Which AI tools** staff use, are allowed to use, and are forbidden to use.
- **Which roles**, and what each does with AI: drafting, summarising, coding, deciding about people, talking to customers.
- **The organisation's AI policy**, if it has one. If not, the training can only teach general good practice, and you should say so.
- **Any duty they are answering to**: an internal rule, a client requirement, a regulation. Name it as they describe it; do not interpret the law for them.

## Levels

| Level | For | They leave able to |
|---|---|---|
| Everyone | All staff | Say what these tools do and do not do; know what must never be pasted into one; check an answer before using it; say when AI was used |
| Daily users | People who use AI in their work | Write a clear request; give context safely; spot a wrong or invented answer; keep a person responsible for the result |
| Deciders | Managers, HR, anyone using AI in decisions about people | Recognise bias and automation bias; know when a human must decide; keep a record |
| Builders | Teams that deploy or configure AI | Test before release; watch it in use; know who to tell when it goes wrong |

Build each level as a short course; join them in a learning path where the "Everyone" course gates the rest.

## What makes it work

- **Their tools, their tasks.** Every example is a task from their job, in the tool they have.
- **Practice.** An activity where the learner improves a weak prompt, or finds the three errors in an AI answer, teaches more than slides about hallucination.
- **The line.** One clear slide per role: allowed, allowed with care, never. Taken from their policy.
- **Honest limits.** These tools can be wrong, can reflect bias, and do not know the organisation's facts unless given them.

## Avoid

- Hype in either direction.
- Claims about what a specific regulation requires of this organisation. Point to their legal or compliance lead.
- Naming a tool as safe for confidential data unless their policy says so.

## Keep it current

AI tools and rules change quickly. Record the date and the tools covered, and put the courses under `keep-training-current` so a change in policy or tooling produces a reviewed update.

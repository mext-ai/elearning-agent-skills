# AI literacy for schools and universities

Needs the MEXTY MCP. For staff in companies, use `ai-literacy-at-work`.

## The framework comes first

Ask which AI literacy framework or curriculum the institution follows: a national or regional one, an international one, or its own. Build to its outcomes with `curriculum-to-learning-path`, one course per outcome, and keep its wording.

If they have none, propose the four strands below as a starting point and say plainly that it is a proposal, not a standard.

## Four strands, by age

| Strand | Ages 4 to 8 | Ages 9 to 12 | Ages 13 to 15 | Ages 16+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What AI is | Machines follow instructions; people have feelings | AI finds patterns in examples | Training, data, models; what a prediction is | How current systems are built and where they fail |
| Using it | Giving clear instructions | Asking good questions; checking an answer | Using tools for study with honesty about it | Choosing tools; judging outputs in a subject |
| Fairness and safety | Private things stay private; ask a grown-up | Mistakes and unfair results come from the examples | Bias, manipulated media, personal data | Rights, accountability, law and society |
| Making | Sort, sequence, spot a pattern | Train a simple classifier by hand | Design a small AI-supported solution to a real problem | A project with testing and a critique |

## How to teach it

- **Unplugged first** for the youngest: sorting games, following and giving instructions. No screens needed to understand a pattern.
- **Play it.** A game where the pupil trains a classifier with good and bad examples teaches bias better than a definition (`homework-to-game`, `learning-game-design`).
- **One project per year** that runs through the units: a real problem, a design, a test, a presentation.
- **Their world.** Examples from what pupils meet: recommendations, filters, voice assistants, homework tools.
- **Age-fit.** For under 13s: no accounts, no personal data, no open chat with an AI system inside an activity.

## Assessment

Check understanding by doing: explain why a result is unfair, improve a prompt, spot the manipulated image, critique a design. See `assessment-design`. Avoid vocabulary tests.

## For teachers

Staff need their own short path before teaching it: what the tools do, the school's rules, how to handle AI in homework and assessment, and how to talk about it honestly when they are learning too.

## Care

- AI changes fast; date every course and plan a yearly review (`keep-training-current`).
- Do not present any AI tool as an authority. The lesson is how to question it.
- Follow the school's policy on which tools pupils may use, and at what age.
